## Program philosophy (read this first)

This program is different from most engineering bootcamps in one important way: **the delivery model is itself AI-first**. Students are supported day-to-day by the AI Coach — you are not the first line of support for most questions.

Your role is to do what AI cannot: build relationships, exercise judgment in ambiguous situations, motivate students through the hard parts, and bring real-world professional context into the room.

**The three things you're optimising for:**

1. **Students ship.** Every week ends with something working and deployed. Anything that blocks shipping is your highest-priority problem.

2. **Students understand what they build.** AI makes it easy to have code without comprehension. The Code Walkthrough assessments are your check on this — but the culture of understanding should permeate everything.

3. **Students develop AI judgment.** Not enthusiasm, not scepticism — judgment. The ability to decide when to trust AI output, when to verify it, and when to reject it. This is the hardest skill to teach and the most valuable to develop.

## Escalation from the AI Coach

The AI Coach Course Coordinator will surface escalation flags to you before your squad check-ins. These flags include:

- Student hasn't shipped by end of Week 1
- Student unable to explain their Week 2 API endpoints (Coordinator flags after reflection check-in)
- Squad missing standups during MVP sprint
- Reflection responses suggesting significant confidence issues or blockers

When you receive a flag, address it in your next scheduled check-in — don't wait for the student to raise it.
