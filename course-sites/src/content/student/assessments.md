## Overview

| Assessment | What it tests | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Code Walkthrough** | Individual comprehension and critical judgment | End of each phase (Weeks 5, 11, 16) |
| **PR History Review** | Engineering discipline and AI collaboration quality | End of Phase 2 + Phase 3 |
| **Portfolio Artifact** | Reflective practice and communication | End of Phase 3 |

---

## Code Walkthrough

**Format:** 10–15 minutes, 1:1 with your Engineering Coach.

Your coach will show you 2–3 sections of your squad's codebase — including sections that may be AI-generated — and ask you to explain them. You won't know in advance which sections are chosen.

**Four things the coach is looking for:**

**Comprehension** — Can you accurately explain what the code does, including inputs, outputs, and side effects?

**Critical evaluation** — Do you evaluate the code, not just describe it? Can you identify potential issues or improvements?

**AI awareness** — Can you describe what the AI generated, what you changed, and what you verified before trusting it?

**System thinking** — Can you explain how this code connects to the rest of the system? What would break if it changed?

**How to prepare:** The best preparation is reading your squad's code — all of it, including sections you didn't write. Make "read before trusting" a habit every time AI generates something. If you can explain it to a squadmate, you can explain it to a coach.

## PR History Review

**Format:** Async review of your PR history by a coach, followed by a 15-minute feedback conversation.

**Assessed at:** End of Phase 2 (Week 11) and Phase 3 (Week 16).

Your coach reviews both PRs you authored and PRs you reviewed.

**Four things the coach is looking for:**

**PR communication** — Do your PRs tell a clear story of what changed and why? Is AI involvement noted?

**Commit discipline** — Does your commit history reflect intentional progress? Can someone follow the narrative from your commits alone?

**AI transparency** — Do you consistently note significant AI contributions and describe what you verified?

**Review quality** — When you review others' code, do you add genuine value? Do you scrutinise AI-generated sections?

**How to prepare:** Start good habits from Week 1. Write meaningful PR descriptions. Note when you used AI and what you changed. Review thoughtfully — a comment that identifies a real issue is worth more than ten approvals.

## Portfolio Artifact

**Format:** Your choice — written case study (1,500–2,500 words), video essay (8–12 minutes), or structured presentation (15 slides + speaker notes).

**Submitted:** End of Phase 3 (Week 15), before Demo Day.

**Audience:** Written for a technical hiring manager or senior engineer.

**Five things it must cover:**

1. **The product** — what you built, who it's for, link to the live app and repo
2. **The build journey** — how the product evolved, major technical decisions, who made them
3. **AI collaboration — what worked** — 2–3 specific examples with real prompts and outcomes
4. **AI collaboration — what didn't work** — 2–3 honest examples of AI failures and what you learned
5. **Who you are as an engineer now** — your mental model for when to use AI, the habits you've developed, what you want to improve

**How to prepare:** The Reflection Interviewer captures your experiences every week. At the end of Phase 3, ask it to surface your key examples from across the program — you'll have much more material than you think.

<div class="callout tip">
  <span class="callout-title">On the portfolio artifact</span>
  <p>The most important dimension is honesty. An artifact that describes only successes is unconvincing. The ones that stand out describe genuine mistakes, moments of confusion, and hard-won lessons — and explain what changed because of them. That's what demonstrates real learning.</p>
</div>
